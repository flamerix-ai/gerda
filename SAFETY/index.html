    Safety architecture — prevention / reaction / recovery¶

    The bot's self-preservation is three concerns that cannot overlap by construction. When you add a mission or a guard, decide which tier it belongs to — putting one concern in the wrong tier is what creates conflicts and dead code.

    Tier Owns the question Scope When it runs Where it lives
    Prevention "Will I choose to commit to something fatal?" self-movement / self-actions only plan-time planner (shared/domain/planner.ts, movement-types.ts), mission-specific guards (e.g. drill floorVerdict)
    Reaction "Is something hurting me right now?" any cause, incl. external (OP /tp, mob, lava) every tick the reflex layer (shared/reflexes/)
    Recovery "Get to safety + respawn" terminal landing on a reflex divert / death bot/capabilities/recover.ts (the RECOVER phase)

    Prevention is plan-time & self-only; reaction is runtime & cause-agnostic; recovery is the landing every reaction diverts to. A given piece of logic belongs to exactly one.

    The rule that prevents duplicated dead code¶

    Missions may PREVENT (proactive, task-specific), but must NOT re-implement REACTION.

    • ✅ The drill refusing to open a shaft floor over water or a fatal drop (floorVerdict in bot/missions/drill/floor-safety.ts) is Prevention — task-specific, stays in the mission. It is a different concern from the global swimOut / waterClutch reflexes (the reaction if prevention fails). That is defense-in-depth, not duplication.
    • ❌ A mission with its own if (health < X) teleport() would duplicate the vitals reflex — that is dead Reaction. Delete it and let vitals own it (the tool reflex owns worn tools).

    If you catch yourself writing a health/air/fall reaction inside a mission, stop — it belongs in a reflex, where it runs for every mission and for the idle bot too.

    Reaction tier: survival vs mission-progress reflexes¶

    Reflexes run before mission logic each tick, first-to-fire preempts. They split by an explicit survival flag on the capability (shared/kernel/capability.ts):

    • survival: true — life-preservation. Runs even when no mission is active (a parked/IDLE bot must not bleed out), via the kernel's idle survival pass. Members: death (respawn), air + swimOut (don't drown), waterClutch (survive falls), vitals (HP floor + distress). If one diverts, RECOVER runs in the idle path too, so an idle bot fully recovers (incl. teleport).
    • mission-progress (no flag) — only meaningful with a task, so gated on st.running: tool (hot-swap), stuck (stuck relative to a nav goal), hazard (feet in the void, lava or fire → RECOVER), hunger, human (yield the mission).

    This is the complete reflex list (src/shared/reflexes/index.ts). Evaluation order is death → vitals → air → waterClutch → swimOut → tool → stuck → hazard → hunger → human.

    This is deliberately not "run everything when idle" — only the five survival reflexes do. A broken tool on an idle bot does nothing; a drowning idle bot saves itself.

    Anti-false-positive: confirm before teleporting an idle bot¶

    A survival divert to teleport-RECOVER must be confirmed, never a one-tick spike:

    • vitals distress requires repeated damage — ≥ cfg.safety.distressMinEvents distinct damage ticks within cfg.safety.distressWindow, not just "lost N HP". A single survived fall is one event → never trips (so an idle bot that took a fall but is now fine doesn't teleport for nothing); sustained drown / lava / suffocation racks up events → escapes.
    • The cfg.safety.minHealth floor fires immediately (you don't reach it from a glitch).
    • RECOVER mitigates in place first (surface via jump) and only teleports once on stable footing — a transient threat stabilises and never escalates.

    Below the reflexes: held keys while the worker tick blocks¶

    Every reflex and every navigator veto runs inside a worker tick. A key that the bot holds stays held while a worker tick runs, so a tick that blocks lets the body walk with no check at all. In RCA I-4 a goto across a hollow-tower roof ran one synchronous extend-replan for about 25 s. Forward and sprint were still held from the previous step, and the bot walked off a 39-block rim. Two layers stop this:

    1. Release before a blocking replan. The executor releases the movement keys before each A* replan (releaseForBlockingWork in shared/nav/core.ts). The drive after the plan presses again what the new route wants. The airborne hold puts the released keys back, because it keeps the heading through a fall.
    2. Movement-key dead-man in main.js. main.js ticks on its own thread. When the worker heartbeat (mc_worker_alive) does not change for DEADMAN_STALE_TICKS (10) main ticks, main.js releases the movement, jump, sprint and sneak keys once for that stall. It also stops the forced sprint and writes a DEADMAN record to the journal, which get_logs shows. diag.main.deadman.stalls counts the stalls and lastStallTick gives the main tick of the last one. main.js cannot see which keys the worker held, so the count includes stalls with no key down; it is not a count of prevented falls. A worker that ticks normally changes the heartbeat every tick, so the dead-man does not act on it. When no worker runs (heartbeat 0), the dead-man does nothing, because a person can hold the keys. A stall also releases a key that a person holds at that time; the person must press it again.

    The decision for layer 2 is deadmanStep (shared/harness/deadman.ts, with its plain-JS twin in harness/safe-io.js). test-ts/harness-contract.test.ts keeps the two equal.

    The two layers stop the walk, not the stall. A long synchronous replan still blocks the worker for its whole length: on the I-4 roof one replan reads about a million cells, about 25 s live. During that time the keys are up and the bot stands still, and no reflex runs. A plan is not cut short by its read count. A cut turned a reachable leg behind a concave pocket (a lava lake, a wide ravine) into a false blocked_no_path; test-ts/nav-replan-safety.test.ts pins that such a leg arrives. The D* Lite engine (cfg.nav.dStar, off by default) searches across worker ticks, at most cfg.nav.dStarStepPops expansions per tick. The executor does not use it for a column leg (src/shared/nav/executor.ts), and the I-4 leg is a column leg, so its replan is one synchronous A* search.

    Prevention tier: one clutch-aware fall predicate¶

    All "will this fall survive?" checks route through one SSOT predicate, canSurviveFall(drop, cap) (core/prim/movement-physics.ts):

    canSurviveFall(drop, cap) = clutch  ∨  fallDamage(drop, waterBelow) ≤ maxFallDamage
    • The planner's cheap drop (dropDownSafe) passes clutch: false — a clutch dive is a separate, costed move (waterDrop), so the cheap drop must not silently offer fatal falls.
    • The executor's fall veto (src/shared/nav/executor.ts stepIsHazardous) passes clutch: true when a bucket is carried (the same gate the planner uses to offer a waterDrop). So the geofence can never veto a fall the water-clutch would save — planner and executor stay in lockstep, and the "with a bucket you can fall from any height" capability is honoured, not contradicted.

    Note the recoverability guard (descent rim-veto, "can I climb back out?") is a different concern and is not clutch-aware — the clutch saves the fall, not the climb-back.

    Prevention tier: known-before-break digging¶

    The drill's DESCEND shaft never breaks the floor under the bot unless it knows what is below. An unloaded or unsensed cell can hide a cave, lava or water, and breaking the floor over it drops the bot in. The rule does not cover EXCAVATE: its void guard (guardVoidCell, src/bot/missions/drill/excavate.ts) reads an unreadable cell as minecraft:stone.

    • The null convention. A senses BlockReader (src/shared/domain/senses.ts) returns null for an UNKNOWN cell. The drill reads its floor through knownReader (src/bot/missions/drill/descend.ts), which keeps an unreadable cell as null instead of masking it as stone.
    • The verdict. floorVerdict (src/bot/missions/drill/floor-safety.ts) returns unknown when the block to break, or any cell of the fall column down to the landing, reads null. floorUnsafe checks every support cell of the body's footprint (footprintAt).
    • The outcome. With no band mined yet, floorUnsafe logs DRILL_UNKNOWN_BELOW and calls skipUnreachableVein (src/bot/missions/drill/select-vein.ts): the bot goes to the next cached vein, or to PAUSED when none remains. It does not go to ERROR. With a band already mined, the unknown floor is the bottom of the shaft: DRILL_DEPTH_DONE, then PAUSED.

    Drowning: one hazard, three layers¶

    Water is not a classifyBlock hazard, so drowning has its own predicate, submergesAt (src/shared/domain/senses.ts): feet cell AND head cell are water. Wading in 1-deep water stays allowed. Each layer sits in its own tier:

    1. Prevention — the navigator veto. stepIsHazardous (src/shared/nav/executor.ts) refuses a step when the step cell, or the landing below it, is submerging. The drill's own floorVerdict water verdict covers the shaft (see the rule above).
    2. Reaction — the swimOut reflex (src/shared/reflexes/swim-out.ts, survival: true). While the bot is submerged and breath is fine, swimOut holds jump and releases forward and sprint until the head clears water. It gives up after cfg.safety.swimOutMaxTicks ticks without a height gain. swimOut does not act in the settled phases RECOVER, PAUSED and ERROR (REFLEX_SETTLED, src/shared/kernel/phase.ts), so a bot that a drill skip left PAUSED in water has only the air reflex. The air reflex (src/shared/reflexes/air.ts) runs first and owns the terminal escape: when the breath fraction drops to cfg.safety.airRecoverPct, air diverts to RECOVER, which holds jump to surface before it teleports (src/bot/capabilities/recover.ts).
    3. Mark and skip — the drill's memory. recordDrowned (src/bot/missions/drill/floor-safety.ts) writes a drowned HazardMarker to mc_world.json through writeHazardMarker (src/shared/domain/world-model.ts). Two events write it: floorUnsafe on a water or void verdict with no band mined (DRILL_WATER, DRILL_VOID), and the DESCEND flooded-column watchdog after cfg.drill.waterContactTicks ticks of water contact with no band mined (DRILL_FLOODED_COLUMN). On the next run, selectDrillVein (src/bot/missions/drill/select-vein.ts) aims the vein at a column of its bounding box outside cfg.drill.hazardRadius of every marker, a peer's included. It drops the vein only when every candidate column is marked. The marker turns one abort into prevention for every later run.

    Client wedges are not a safety-tier concern¶

    After a headless death the client process can stay without a loaded world while the bridge still answers. That is a process fault, not game state, so it is handled host-side by scripts/ops/wedge_watchdog.py (./gerda watchdog): detect, disconnect and reconnect, and optionally relaunch. It never touches the kernel or bundle. Needs a running bot:

    ./gerda watchdog --port 55545 --once                      # one check: exit 0 healthy, 3 wedged
./gerda watchdog --port 55545 --server localhost:25565    # watch; reconnect on a confirmed wedge

    A relaunch of the instance happens only with --auto-relaunch <instance>; without it the watchdog prints the relaunch command and keeps watching.
